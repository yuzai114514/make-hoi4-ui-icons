# TNO style

Inspect the target TNO version and branch before applying this guide. The local translated guide is explicitly marked as possibly outdated.

For focuses, use the shared [design references](focus-design.md) for composition, material layers, lettering, and variation within a branch.

## Direct the image

- Make the subject readable but conceptually inventive; communicate an event or condition rather than showing a bare symbol alone.
- Reduce saturation, darken the scene, and increase contrast.
- Apply heavy shadow, bevel, controlled gradient, and directional lighting to give objects weight.
- Let a focal object overlap or break the focus frame when that improves energy and meaning.
- Split materials where independent editing helps, preserving coherent depth and perspective across the scene.
- For national spirits, favor an immediately legible subject and usually omit a decorative focus frame.

## Avoid

- Avoid TFR-like bright neon, uniformly high saturation, and glossy modern-thumbnail energy.
- Avoid a lone cross, gear, portrait, or building when a stronger scene can express the concept.
- Avoid oversized low-resolution subjects, reused signature material across unrelated icons, and flat pasted edges.
- Do not force frame breaking when the target branch uses another TNO substyle.

## Technical boundary

Use the exact canvas sizes in [icon-types.md](icon-types.md); derive frame geometry and dimensions of unlisted types from the target project. Treat electronic/glow tutorials as optional substyle evidence, not as permission to override the dark, desaturated branch reference.
